How it works

You get a probability question about dice, cards, birthdays or coin flips. Say how likely you think the event is. Once you lock in your answer, the computer actually performs the experiment (rolls the dice, deals the cards) and tells you whether the event happened.

You are scored with the Brier score: the square of the gap between your probability and what happened (1 if it happened, 0 if not). Lower is better. Saying 50% every time always scores 0.25. Say 70% and the event happens: 0.09. Say 70% and it does not: 0.49.

One experiment is luck, so the game also shows the exact true probability and the noise-free score. Over many questions you learn whether your gut feelings are well calibrated: when you say 80%, does it really happen about 80% of the time?
